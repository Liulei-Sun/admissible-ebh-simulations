\subsection{Learning asymmetric weights}

We used $K=200$ one-sided Gaussian mean hypotheses at level $\alpha=0.05$,
with $|N^c|\in\{20,30\}$. In each replication, $N^c$ was selected uniformly
among subsets of the required size and
$\{X_{t,i}:t=1,\ldots,60,\ i=1,\ldots,K\}$ were mutually independent, with
$X_{t,i}\sim N(\mu_i,1)$, $\mu_i=0$ under a null, and $\mu_i=0.45$ under an
alternative. The first $20$ observations were used only to learn weights and
the last $40$ only to test. With $\eta=0.45$, we formed
\[
 E_{i,S}=\exp\left\{\eta\sum_{t\in S}X_{t,i}
                -\frac{|S|\eta^2}{2}\right\}.
\]
The testing e-values and ordinary testing-only eBH set were kept fixed across
all training-data conditions.

Writing $s_i=E_{i,\{1,\ldots,20\}}$ and taking $\gamma=1$, the two learned
constant-free weight systems, for every nonempty $A\subseteq\mathcal K$ and
$i\in A$, were
\[
 \lambda^A_{i,1}=\lambda^A_{i,\mathrm{nor}}
   =\frac{s_i}{\sum_{j\in A}s_j},\qquad
 \lambda^A_{i,2}=\lambda^A_{i,\mathrm{adj}}
   =\frac1K+\frac{K-|A|}{K}\frac{s_i}{\sum_{j\in A}s_j},
 \qquad \lambda^A_{0,\ell}=0.
\]
Conditional on the independent training data, these weights are fixed and the
testing e-values remain valid, giving conditional and hence unconditional FDR
control for the reported learned procedures.

For persistent medicine-specific measurement error, we drew one
$\varepsilon_i\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,0.1)$ per medicine and replication,
independently of all frozen data, and replaced the training observations by
\[
  X^{\star}_{t,i}=X_{t,i}+\varepsilon_i,
  \qquad t=1,\ldots,20.
\]
Thus the training sufficient statistic changes by $20\varepsilon_i$, while the
testing statistic is unchanged.  We learned weights proportional to
$\exp\{0.45\sum_{t=1}^{20}X^{\star}_{t,i}\}$ on the training sample, then applied
the learned procedures to the independent testing e-values. We also considered the
signed-square condition $X^\star_{t,i}=X_{t,i}^2\operatorname{sgn}(X_{t,i})$.
For comparison, the naive pooled procedure inserted the contaminated training
sum and clean testing sum into the clean 60-observation e-value formula.

Procedure 1 reports a globally maximum-cardinality certified set. Procedure 2
reports a maximum-cardinality certified set among those containing ordinary
testing-only eBH. Among sets of optimal cardinality, both procedures maximize
the sum of the testing e-values; any remaining numerical tie is resolved by the
fixed one-thread solver configuration with solver seed zero.

The persistent-error variance was fixed at $0.1$. The same Gaussian base data,
truth labels, testing e-values, ordinary-eBH prefixes, and testing-only results
were used across the training-data conditions. The $R=1000$ results combine two
independently seeded batches of $500$ replications. The second batch was added
to improve Monte Carlo precision, while the first batch was left unchanged.
Every MCSE is the sample standard deviation of the 1,000 replication-level TPR
or FDP values divided by $\sqrt{1000}$.

Table~\ref{tab:learned-weight-power} reports the final results. The testing-only procedures are identical across the training-data conditions because their e-values and rejection rules use only the frozen clean testing sample. Under the signed-square distortion, the naive pooled mean $\overline{\mathrm{eBH}}$ has empirical FDRs 0.0710 and 0.0580 for 20 and 30 nonnulls. Under persistent medicine-specific noise, its empirical FDRs are 0.0708 (95\% Monte Carlo interval [0.0662,0.0754]) and 0.0581 (95\% Monte Carlo interval [0.0547,0.0615]). This illustrates why the contaminated training observations should not be pooled into e-values as if they were clean.

With persistent noise, Learned asymmetric closed eBH 1 has TPRs 0.1768 and 0.2038, while Learned asymmetric closed eBH 2 has TPRs 0.2016 and 0.2857. This pattern is consistent with persistent shifts corrupting the score ranking, while the $1/K$ floor in $\lambda_{\mathrm{adj}}$ limits reliance on those corrupted scores. The globally largest reported set from Procedure 1 contains ordinary testing eBH in 338/1000 and 152/1000 replications. Procedure 2 contains ordinary testing eBH by construction and did so in 1000/1000 and 1000/1000 replications. The testing samples and testing-only procedures are unchanged across all three training-data conditions.
